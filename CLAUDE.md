## The project in three lines

Forme NURBS is a hard-surface modelling app (PC first, iPad later): an object is a list of features (Cube, Cylinder…) combined in order (Add = union, Carve = cut), built as exact B-rep solids by **OpenCascade** and shown as its mesh with its B-rep edges. The UI looks like Forme's « Établi » interface (floating islands, tool cards, one inspector, history strip), in English, dark, with the white accent.

11. **Direct fillet modifiers and tension** (asked 19 nov., 6 h 25: "En edge comme en point / CTRL maintenu : chamfer mode / SHIFT maintenu : tension set"; captures only). During a fillet drag (`update_round_drag(at, ctrl, shift)`): Ctrl held makes it a chamfer for this drag only (the top island's option stays); Shift held sets the tension instead of the size (on an edge fillet: up fuller, down flatter, 0.004 per point; on a sketch / path corner: the conic's middle follows the pointer along the line from its ends' middle to the corner, `corner_rho_at`). The tension is a conic's rho (0.05 to 0.95; 0 = circular, whose rho is `circle_rho(θ)`, 0.414 at 90°): `Round.tension` for a fillet step, `Profile.tension[i]` for a corner; the op window has a Tension slider and Circular. A corner: `Cut.rho`, a rational quadratic (`cut_points`, `CutKind::Conic`, `focc_prism_cut` ck 3). An edge fillet: after OpenCascade's fillet, `Shape::fillet_tension(&[(tag, rho)])` (`focc_fillet_tension`, several fillets in one go, each its tension) replaces each round face by a conic one: a cylinder by the conic swept along its axis (`Geom_SurfaceOfLinearExtrusion`), a torus (a fillet along a circle: a boss's top or foot, a rounded corner of a chain) by the conic turned round its axis (`Geom_SurfaceOfRevolution`, the axis X × Y: OpenCascade's torus frames may be left-handed); faces built by hand, the contact edges kept (their pcurves moved), each end section remade as the conic moved onto its plane. An end may lie on a flat face (square or oblique to the axis), on two flat faces (a chamfer and a side: the vertex between them moved onto the new surface along the edge leaving there, `GeomAPI_IntCS`), on another conic face of the same tension (a mitre: the section's own plane), or on a **sphere corner** where three straight fillets of one tension meet: the corner is remade as a degenerate rational Bezier patch through the three new ends (poles P P P / Mb K Mc / Q Ma R, weights wi wj, K = Mb + Ma − Q; a sphere's eighth again at the circle's rho), tangent to all three (a fully rounded box takes tension, test `tension_rounded_box`, shot `tension-box`). **Two fillet sizes** (19 nov., captures only; test `tension_two_sizes`, shot `tension-two`): a fillet face that is neither a cylinder nor a torus (OpenCascade's fillet along a conic edge: the top edges' fillet made after the vertical ones') takes its tension as a rational BSpline (`ten_general`: the conic across between its two contact edges, cubic along through 33 samples, `ten_rows`); a **corner torus** where one fillet runs round another's rounded edge (the top edges' fillet round a vertical edge, whichever fillet owns it: `TenCorner`, between a cylinder on its axis and a flat face square to it, its ends on the other fillet's cylinders) is remade with the section of the fillet whose ends it joins (a circle when that one has none) and the run of the cylinder's new section (a conic when its fillet has tension), its edge on the flat face following (64 samples); both orders give the same shape. **A vertical fillet smaller than the top one** (3 under 5, either order; captures only, test `tension_two_sizes`, shot `tension-small`): OpenCascade makes a spline corner (a face of the vertical fillet) between the block's side, the top fillet running on and the end of the other top fillet. A general face's contacts and ends are told apart by its arcs (a fillet's ends are circles), else by the edges it shares with its own fillet's faces, else the longer pair are the contacts. Its tension goes over evenly along it from one end's to the other's: at an end on a face made conic, that face's (a cylinder's or torus's, any tension); at a circle shared with a round face left so, the circle (kept, `general_keeps`, `edge_is_arc`); else its own. A contact on a cylinder made conic is followed (`TenFollow`, `ten_follow_point`: each point moved onto the new surface at the same place along the axis and the same direction out from it, the conic in the plane through both contacts and the other face's normal, tangent to both; its new edge is the new surface's row, its curve on the cylinder's new face through the samples' places there), and a face of a fillet not asked is remade that way too (`follower`: round where free). Kept edges get a tolerance covering the samples' error measured between them (`cover`, 160 points). A conic tighter than the fillet running round it (+10 %, `conic_min_radius`) leaves its cylinder round and says so (`tight`: its tightest radius, the size, the tensions that work); the build then makes it at the nearest tension that works (`Model.tension_limits`, encoded last) and the app takes it as a fillet's size (`clamp_rounds`, toast "tension 0.52 is its fullest here"); a 5 mm vertical fillet under a 3 mm top one works from 0.365 to 0.515. What cannot (an end on a round face of another kind, a three-edge corner other than three equal straight fillets) stays circular and the model lists it ("tension on n of m faces"). In the build (`build::tension`, the round step): a blended fillet (`blend`) rebuilds the run circular, then every tension of the run is put back with its own, in one call; when that loses a tension that took before (`Stage.conic`) or this step's own, the fillet made on its own next to the conic faces is taken instead if it loses fewer (made at its size only: `round(.., search: false)`, since a smaller one would not be taken; any size search stops after 12 tries (a count, so the answer does not depend on the load) or 6 s, as OpenCascade can take half a minute per try next to a face curved tighter than the fillet). Limit, geometric (not the algorithm): a chamfer or fillet running along a tensioned fillet's end (its chain continues round the conic) cannot be bigger than the conic's tightest radius of curvature: for a flatter one (rho < 0.5) at its ends, 2 r w² with w = rho / (1 − rho) at 90° (on the test part 0.57 mm at tension 0.2 with a 4.6 mm fillet, 2.3 at 0.35), for a fuller one in its middle; it stops there as any fillet too big. **A corner closing on a point** (19 nov., captures only; test `tension_point_corner`, shot `tension-point`): a 2 mm fillet on the top front edge, then a 3 mm one on the top -X edge: OpenCascade carries the second round the first one's end (an arc tighter than it) and down the front vertical edge, its corner a three-sided spline face whose contact on the -X side shrinks to a point (a degenerated edge). `ten_general` takes a degenerated edge's side as the contacts (the conic rows all start at that point; `ten_rows` gives a one-point row the others' knots), its new pcurve is the new surface's row at that point. The two cylinders' sections there are a little off a plane (0.011 mm): taken on the plane through their ends and middle (up to 1 % of their chord, only where they meet a general face of the same fillet), and the general face's end rows are those cylinders' new conics exactly (`TenFace.endc`), the 2.5° turn of their tangents spread smoothly over the run (each kept square to its face's normal), else the shading wrinkles next to the end. With Blend corners on, the circular fillet is blended with the first one (a mitre, no vertical run); with tension it is made on its own. Tests `edge_tension`, `edge_tension_test_part`, `corner_tension`, `tension_boss_ring`, `tension_boss_foot`, `tension_run`, `tension_oblique_ends`, `tension_next_fillets` (a smaller fillet next to a conic one now takes its own tension), `tension_after_corner`, `tension_rounded_box`, `tension_two_sizes`, `tension_probe`, `tension_probe2` (four orders × four tension pairs), `tension_probe3` (ignored, print; probe 3 takes `FORME_T1` / `FORME_T2` / `FORME_R1` / `FORME_R2`, the top's then the vertical's, and `FORME_VFIRST=1`); shots `tension`, `tension-box`, `tension-two`, `tension-small`.

## Code map

- `crates/forme-occt`: our thin C layer over OpenCascade (`cpp/forme_occt.cpp` + safe Rust wrapper). Primitives, transforms, booleans, fillets / chamfers by edge index, meshing. Every OpenCascade exception is caught and returned as `Err`; only `opencascade::handle<T>` is used (the `Handle_T` aliases break under MSVC). **Face tags**: each face carries an `i64` tag that booleans, transforms, fillets and chamfers carry to the faces they make (`carry_tags`, history `Modified`); new fillet / chamfer faces get the `fresh` tag. `Shape::topo(defl, angle)` returns in one buffer the faces (tag, surface kind, plane point / normal, axis, radius), the mesh with a face index per triangle, and the edges (index, the two faces, kind, seam, polyline). OpenCascade is built by `occt-sys` (feature `builtin`, cached in `target/OCCT`) or taken from `FORME_OCCT_ROOT`.
  Quads: `quad_topo()` (each face's wires in order, each edge's ends, kind and samples), `edge_on_face(face, edge, reversed, t)` (points and the face's uv along an edge), `face_eval(face, uv)` (points and outward normals).
  Faces: `inset_face(face, d)` (BRepOffsetAPI_MakeOffset, needs TKOffset; the wires are rebuilt from fresh edges on their 3D curves, else the booleans fail on a face with holes; d = 0 copies the face), `extrude(v)`, `imprint(tool)` (Splitter: the face under the tool cut along its outline, the inside takes the tool's tag), `face_distance(face, p)`. In a boolean, a face made of faces of both shapes (flush faces merged) keeps the part's tag, not the tool's.
- `crates/nurbs-kernel`: the document and the kernel.
  - `doc.rs`: `Doc { features }`, `Feature { id, name, op, prim, origin, rot, visible, material, round }`. A feature's origin is the centre of its base; it rises along its local +Y. Saved as JSON `.fnurbs`. A feature with `round: Some(Round { size, chamfer, edges })` is a fillet / chamfer step on what is built before it (`is_solid()` is false; its `op`, `prim`, `rot` are unused). Each edge is an `EdgeRef { faces: [tag, tag], at }`: the packed tags of the two faces it joins, and its middle point to choose between edges joining the same two faces (`build::resolve`). The fillet's new faces carry the tag `(fillet id, 0)`, so clicking them selects the fillet. A feature with `push: Some(Push { face: FaceRef { tag, at }, inset, depth })` is an Inset / Extrude step (label "Inset" when inset > 0, else "Extrude"; `Doc::add_push`). A sketch is `Prim::Profile { x, y, z }` (its outline's box, its height) with `profile: Some(Profile { points, smooth })`, the points in the unit square of local X / Z (resizing stretches it); a curve point is a quadratic curve between the middles of its two sides (`doc::pieces`, same as `focc_prism`).
  - `model.rs`: what the kernel sends back: mesh, `tri_face`, faces with their `FaceTag { feature, sub }` (box sub: 0 +X, 1 −X, 2 top, 3 bottom, 4 +Z, 5 −Z; cylinder: 0 side, 1 top, 2 bottom), edges, errors; binary encode / decode.
  - `build.rs` (feature `occ`): features applied in order (a Carve with nothing before it is ignored), tool solids cached by feature hash, stages by prefix hash, a sketch's faces tagged 1 top, 2 bottom, 3 + its outline piece id (`sub_of`), `STEP what:id:hash` written on stderr before each risky step. `push`: the face found by tag and nearest point (`find_face`), inset, then imprinted (depth 0), fused or cut as a prism (carving starts 0.1 mm outside the face); its new faces tagged `(step, 1)` for the inset or the end, `(step, 2…)` for the sides.
  - `quads.rs` (feature `occ`): the quad converter. `convert(shape, target)`: each face's loops in a (u, v) map scaled to millimetres (`FMap`, the outline counter-clockwise; for a closed edge the pcurve that keeps the loop going is taken, `Span.pc`), cut into regions with four corners (outline turns > 25°) by straight cuts that stop at the first line they meet (a T), on a node when one is close. In order: several holes kept apart by a cut across the face (`separate`); each hole joined by a left and a right ray; then per region a ray along a side of an inner corner, a ray from an inward arc of more than 120° (where it runs along u or v), a ray straight in where a straight side meets a rounded one, the best chord while more than 4 corners (`score`: corner counts, angles, no sliver, no cut brushing past the outline, `clear`), a disc (a round loop with no corner) cut into a centre square and four side patches by paths through new inner points (`disc`, `split_path`), forced corners while fewer (where a straight side meets a rounded one, else the middle of the longest run). ## Rules that are easy to break

- **No SDF, ever, on this branch**: the part on screen is always the kernel's mesh. While the kernel computes, the previous mesh stays, with the moved or created shape drawn as an outline.
- Picking and selection go through the kernel's faces and their tags (`Model::faces[f].tag`), never through a field or a guess from the shape list.
- A tag is `(feature << 16) | sub`; any new primitive must tag all its faces in `nurbs_kernel::build::sub_of`. Edges are never named by their index in OpenCascade's edge map (it changes with every build): always by the tags of their two faces.
- Both sides of the kernel protocol (`proto.rs`, `model.rs` encode / decode) change together; the kernel process is rebuilt with the app.
- The kernel builds the history island by island (plan point 35) and must give the same part as one history: a step that looks at the whole part (a simplify, a search among all faces, a run of fillets) goes through the island helpers (`gap`, `strangers_join`, `joins_here`); check with `islands_live`, `islands_battery` (also with `FORME_BATTERY=mirror-first` and `mirror-last`) and `islands_probe` on saved scenes.
- A face that must keep its outline on a surface it shares with its neighbour (an inset left as a line) is named to the C layer by `forme_occt::keep_lines` in `build`: any new kind of drawn line that is not two pieces of one tag goes there too, or the next union anywhere takes it away.
- Never commit `target/` or `shots/replay/frames/`. Never run `cargo fmt` on the whole tree.
